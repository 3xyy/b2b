When we started the **Bounce Back Project**, we set out to answer a simple
question: what happens to a tennis ball after it stops bouncing? For most of the
330 million produced each year, the answer is a landfill — where they can take
more than 400 years to break down.

Today we're thrilled to share a milestone: **over 100,000 tennis balls collected**,
with more than 30,000 already rehomed.

## Where the balls go

- **Schools** — slipped onto chair legs to cut classroom noise and protect floors.
- **Animal shelters** — as enrichment and chew toys for dogs.
- **Assisted living centers** — as walker glides that make hallways safer and quieter.

Every month, our volunteers collect between 3,000 and 5,000 used balls from local
clubs and academies.
